
The rapid expansion of sequencing data in genomics and healthcare presents both opportunities and challenges, particularly in the study of bacterial exotoxins. Since the early 2000s, high-throughput sequencing has generated vast amounts of unlabeled genomic data, including sequences encoding exotoxins. Bacterial exotoxins play key roles in therapeutics, such as pain management, bacterial infection treatments, and neurodegenerative disease research. However, experimental (wet lab) research on exotoxins is labor-intensive and time-consuming, creating a \replace{need }{opportunity }for computational approaches \replace{for}{to} further research \add{faster}.

Machine learning has emerged as a powerful tool for analyzing these complex sequences, but existing classification models primarily focus on binary distinctions (e.g., toxin vs. non-toxin, exotoxin vs. endotoxin). This study addresses a critical gap by developing predictive models that classify exotoxins by functional type and target specificity (vertebrate vs. invertebrate).

Using a non-redundant dataset of 1,067 sequences, we developed ExoType and ExoTarget, two machine learning models leveraging protein embeddings generated by large language models with T5 architecture. ExoType, an SVM-based predictor, classifies exotoxins based on their functional type, while ExoTarget, a Random Forest model, predicts their biological targets. Both models integrate sequence-derived embeddings (MMseqs2) and structure-based embeddings (AlphaFold2) to improve classification performance.

Our models achieved Matthews Correlation Coefficients (MCC) of 70\% for ExoType and 67\% for ExoTarget, demonstrating the potential of machine learning and large language models in advancing bacterial exotoxin classification.
